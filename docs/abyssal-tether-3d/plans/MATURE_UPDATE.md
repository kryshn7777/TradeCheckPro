# The mature update: death scenes, jetpack, wrist map, water, wind, remains, voices, a scarier Dresser

Owner's brief (2026-11-05): "make the villain more scary ... a death animation ... the game is going to be definitely R-rated: bloodstains, a lot of skeletons ... the wind whooshing ... trim the head of the skitterlings ... water flowing here and there ... a better Kokoro ... a 3D hologram map in his watch ... a jetpack with really low fuel."
Everything below is in the free build (Chapters I and II); each part has its own test in `tools/abyssal-tether-3d/tests/` and is listed in `run-tests.mjs`. Nothing here was played on a headset; the Quest numbers are arithmetic or the desktop software renderer.

| Part | Where | What it does | Switch |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Death scenes** | `sim/deathScene.js`, `render/deathOverlay.js`, `MasonRigView.cine`, `game.js _beginDeathScene / _applyDeathCam` | A fall / landing / ironwork / the Dresser: the camera drops to a body's height (desktop rolls onto its side), the **eyelids close slowly** with one flutter, black. A skitterling swarm: the same with **blood on the lens**. **The Mason**: you are thrown down, the world goes out, **two red eyes ignite in the dark and come closer**, the jaw opens past reason, it lunges over the camera and **bites** (blood splashes the lens and runs), black, the sound of chewing. 4 / 3.7 / 6.6 s; a press skips after 1.7 / 1.4 / 2.8 s; the CONTINUITY restore and the death panel wait for it (`game.restoreClock`). Sound + caption per beat. | comfort: `reducedEffects` = no shake, half roll |

**Rating.** The build now shows blood, remains and a graphic death scene on purpose: `store/LISTING.md` and `RELEASE.md` section 16 were rewritten for it (expect Mature 17+ / PEGI 16-18); keep blood and bones out of the key art.

**Debug API.** `AT3D.debug.death.play('impact' | 'fall' | 'hazard' | 'creature' | 'dressed', { killer: 'mason' | 'skitter' })`, `.hold(t)`, `.state()`; `AT3D.debug.jet.state() / fuel(v) / ignite() / cans()`.

**Not verified.** Nothing on a headset (the VR wrist gesture, the lids and the Mason's face in stereo, the jetpack's feel); the sound was checked by measurement and the strict mock context, not by ear (voices and every new effect); the water and the remains were only seen in the desktop software renderer.
