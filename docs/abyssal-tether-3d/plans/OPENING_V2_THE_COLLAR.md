> slowly descending him. With my voices that the team is here, your job is to go and do this, your job is to... In this story, we

This replaces shots 1-4 of [OPENING_CINEMATIC.md](OPENING_CINEMATIC.md) (the lantern alone on a chain) and re-aims shots 5-9. Everything

## 1. The story in one breath

**What the player is told.** Elias Wren is **the Line**: the rope rescuer of Expedition 42, the technician who goes first and rigs the
rope so other people can come down safely. The real job, relighting the Threshold's lamp so the Ember can be kindled, belongs to
**the Lighters**, Perennial's lighting crew, who are four hours behind him. His job is small and safe: the four hoist drones of
**the Collar** (the station over the shaft) lower his cradle 62 m to **Lamp Zero** (the Threshold's lamp; the game's depth 0), he clears
the fallen stone off the ring points, rings the bell so the Lighters can hear where he is, and waits on his line. *Clean the place.*

**What he does.** He rings the bell. The bell opens the seal under the chimney (it always did: *Its toll opens the way down. Only down.*),
his line is released from above, and he goes down, deeper than anyone was supposed to go, below the reach of the line, the drones and
the plan. The radio is alarmed. Chapter I becomes *get down, and find a way*.

**What is true** (never said aloud in Chapter I; the bible's §2.3 team truth, unchanged): there is no Lighters' schedule that matters,
because this is the forty-first time. The drop was the plan. The work order reads **PAYLOAD 1 · RETURN 0**. The drones' scan that
"calibrates" him is the measuring; the place at the bottom is cut to that measure. The release is commanded from the top, by the only
person on the channel who is not surprised.

### 1.3 Names

- **The Collar**: the station at the shaft's mouth (white / graphite composite, cold LED strips, a launch deck around a 22 x 22 m
  opening, an iris over it). It sits on the Cap; the iris is the only way through the Cap.
- **Lamp Zero**: the Threshold's lamp, depth 0 m in the game's own depth readout (the wrist, the comms depths). The Collar's deck is 62 m
  above it.

- **The Lighters**: the relighting crew. (Not the Lamplighters of Chapter II's Lamphold, a Lanternwright order; the rhyme is on purpose.)
- **The cradle**: a small caged harness platform: a solid floor plate, four corner posts, a hip rail, a lit rope spool, his lantern on
  the rail hook. Stencil **CRADLE 01**.

## 4. A headset (comfort)

- **The cradle is the reference frame.** From 0 to the lantern going out you stand in the cradle: your head is the cradle's floor point
  + your own eye height over your own floor (seated works: the offset is the height over the floor, as before). The floor plate is
  solid; the cage is around you; the lantern hangs 0.62 m in front of you, below your eyes.
- **Translation only, never rotation**: the rig's yaw is the spawn yaw for the whole film; head tracking is always live.
- **Vertical only, at <= 1.0 m/s**: the lift (<= 0.75 m/s), the descent (0.9 m/s), the landing (eased to rest).
- **Three vantage changes, each behind black**: the two descent cuts (fade-through-black dips 32.4-33.6 and 41.4-42.6, black at the
  jump) and the move to the spawn at **81.0** (the screen is black from the lantern going out until the title).
- No letterbox, no shake, no flash (as before). The title is world-locked 6 m ahead of the spawn vantage, as before.
- **The height beat**: the scan line sweeps past your real head at 15.4-17.6 (it is drawn in the world, so it crosses your eyes at your
  own height). The game has no separate height calibration system; this is a story beat, not a measurement.
- The Mason's lunge target is pushed 1 m farther from the vantage in a headset (you are 0.6 m from the lantern, not 3.2 m).

## 7. The fall (in play): the minimal robust mechanism

**Decision: no new physics, no new level geometry, no scripted body motion.** The S1 objective already is the bell (`obj-s1`, verb
`bell`); ringing it already opens `seal-s1`, the grating across the chimney under the Threshold, and the route already drops through
it to the S2 checkpoint (y -21.6): the bots, the par times, the splits, the checkpoints and respawns all already know this fall.
What changes is what it **means**:

1. The film and the first radio lines frame the bell as a harmless job (*ring the bell for the Lighters*).
2. `objective:done` (verb `bell`) triggers **the fall**: Vesper *"Line release commanded."*, Tick *"I didn't send that."*, Odessa
   *"Easy, Elias. Grab a ring and go with it."* The lines are section-independent, so a player who drops at once still hears them.
3. The first entry of S2 plays the surprise (Tick, Maren, Odessa calm, Elias).

It is presentation only (comms is keyed on bus events and changes nothing in the simulation), so it cannot break a route, a bot, a par
time, a split, a checkpoint, a respawn or a save; each line plays once per run (the director books a line when it triggers); a
Continue that starts in S2 hears only the S2 lines (still true: he is under Lamp Zero). Non-lethal: the S1 -> S2 drop is the verified
route (verify.mjs V1 seal checks, the route bots).

Not done (out of scope, noted for later): a visible line falling past the player at the release, a sound for it in the game mix.

**The facilityKit contract** (another agent builds the models; this branch codes against it): `buildDrone(opts) -> THREE.Group`
(origin at the body centre; a child named `/rotor|prop|fan/i` spins about its local Y if present), `buildCradle() -> THREE.Group`
(origin at the floor plate's top centre, +Y up, the lantern hook on the -Z rail), `buildCollar() -> {group, dronePads, cageAnchor,
irisCenter, lights, setIris?}`, `FACILITY`. The film's choreography lives in `COLLAR` (the sim cannot import three.js); at merge,
either the kit's pads / cage anchor are placed at `COLLAR`'s, or `COLLAR` is edited to the kit's (one constants block; the view logs a
console warning once when they differ by more than 0.5 m).
