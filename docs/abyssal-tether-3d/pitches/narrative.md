# ABYSSAL TETHER: Five-Chapter Plan · Pitch: "NARRATIVE / HORROR DRIVEN"

> Role: lead game designer. Angle: the story is told by the architecture, by sound and by the rules of the game
> itself. Every chapter has its own mood and colour. Dread comes from what you cannot see. The endings change what the
> whole descent meant. Date: 2026-10-19. Status: pitch for owner review. Target: Meta Horizon Store, WebXR PWA, free demo
> (Chapter I) plus a $9.99 in-app unlock. 5 chapters, about 2 h 40 min for a first-time player. Full 3D movement, no
> comfort options, three.js r170, no Unity. Quality before speed (owner, 2026-10-19).

---

## 0. The pitch on one page

**Logline.** Each time you die in the Underspire, you wake at the last lamp you passed, and the body you died in stays
behind. You descend two and a half kilometres of living cathedral on two tethers. The thing hunting you is made of
everyone the light has ever left behind, and part of it is you.

**Tagline** (already carved at the start of the game): *Trust the ring, not the floor.*

**The idea in one sentence.** The game's own respawn is the curse at the centre of the story. Checkpoints are votive
lamps that wake you. Deaths leave stone bodies behind. Those bodies are what the statues are, what the walls are made of,
and what the Mason is made of. The player lives the twist through the game's rules long before any image confirms it.

**Totals.** 2,700 m of descent, about **158 minutes** for a median first-time player on Normal, deaths included (range
146–170). **Three endings**, all of them performed with your own hands: *Unkindled*, *Kept*, and the secret *Answered*.
**25 Names** to collect, plus your own.

**Why narrative-driven is the right bet for this product.**
1. **VR horror sells through stories people retell.** "The kneeling statue had my gloves, and when I turned my lantern
   away it stood up" is a clip, a review line and a word-of-mouth hook. That moment ends the free demo (§6).
2. **It spends most of the budget on the systems we already have.** Respawn, checkpoints, lanterns, plaques, statues
   and a sound-hunting predator all exist today. The story recasts them. There are only three large new systems
   (moving statues, water, stillness), and each carries one chapter.
3. **$9.99 needs a reason to finish, not just a reason to start.** A mystery with three endings, and a collectible that
   unlocks the best one, gives players a reason to finish and then replay.
4. **It fits alongside the other pitches.** This document is a story spine and a dread grammar. The set-piece and
   systems pitches' mechanics (live anchors, machinery, swarms) can be placed inside these chapters (§15).

## 1. The story (team bible; the player never reads this)

### 1.1 Canon rules (fixed; every chapter obeys them)

1. **The Still Flame wakes those who carry its light.** A Lanternwright's lantern is kindled from it. Die, and you wake
   at the last votive lamp you passed. *(Gameplay: checkpoints are lamps.)*
2. **What the Flame lets go of, it keeps.** The body you died in stays where it fell and turns to stone. *(Gameplay:
   remnants, §3.2.)*
3. **The kept are blind, and they are not still in the dark.** Out of the light they walk, slowly, toward the lamp that
   left them. *(Gameplay: the Kept, Chapter III. It is also why the Mason comes for lantern-bearers.)*
4. **Too long in the Flame's own light, or beside a lamp kindled straight from it (the Mark Lamp), and you are
   stilled:** whole, forever, not woken. A pilgrim who is stilled
   wakes again at the Threshold, with every votive cold. *(Gameplay: stillness, Chapter V. Story: the loop you are in.)*
5. **Nothing wakes what is not kindled.** Put out your lantern and you can truly die. *(Story: the Unkindled, Chapter IV,
   and ending A.)*

### 1.2 The Lanternwrights and what became of them

An order of builders found a flame at the bottom of the world that never moves, makes no sound and is never consumed:
the **Still Flame**. Its gift was waking. For centuries they built the Underspire downward toward it: *"THE LAMP DOES
NOT RISE TO MEET US. WE GO DOWN TO IT."* They died freely, because dying did not matter. *"TRUST THE RING, NOT THE
FLOOR."*

Their dead piled up as stone. At first they stood them up as statues, robed, lantern raised: the statues in Chapter I.
Then there were too many, so they **dressed them into blocks and built with them.** The Underspire is made of its
builders. *"IT WEARS OUR WORK."*

The order split three ways. Each faction's belief is one chapter, and one ending:

| Faction | Belief | Where you meet them | Their ending |
|---|---|---|---|
| **The Keepers** (the orthodox) | Keep the dead lit and still, and never answer them. They wrote the plaques in Chapter I. | Chapter I, Chapter III (they built the Keeping) | **B: Kept**. You kneel, lantern raised, and become what every statue is. |
| **The Lampholders** | Live with your dead and name them. They lit lamps below the Mark in defiance. *"KNOCK, AND BE KNOWN."* | Chapter II (their town) | **C: Answered**. You name the dead and they lie down. |
| **The Unkindled** | Put out your light and rest. | Chapter IV (they sleep at the bottom of the Mere) | **A: Unkindled**. You put out the Flame itself. |

### 1.3 The Still Flame's secret

It does not give life. It **keeps a moment and hands it back**, so every waking is a copy, and the original stays
behind as stone. The more the Lanternwrights relied on it, the more of themselves they left in the dark. The Flame is
the source of the order's immortality, of its architecture and of its predator.

### 1.4 The Mason's origin

The deep courses of the Underspire were laid from the oldest dressed dead. When the lamps along the deep course failed,
the stones woke in the dark (rule 3). Packed together, they moved **as one**. *"THE MASONS WENT DOWN TO MEND THE DEEP
COURSE. THE WALL MENDED ITSELF"*: it took the menders in too. The Mason is blind because stone eyes see nothing. It hunts
by sound because stone carries sound (*"THE STONE HAS BEEN LISTENING"*). It knocks from inside the walls because each
body in it is still knocking to be named (*"DO NOT ANSWER WHAT KNOCKS FROM INSIDE THE WALL"*). It comes for
lantern-bearers because every piece of it walks toward the light that left it. Its crown of sensory fronds is a ring of
raised, empty arms.

### 1.5 Who you are

You are a pilgrim, and not for the first time. Each time a pilgrimage ends in stillness, the Threshold lamp wakes you
again (rule 4). The game's first frame is a waking, and it looks exactly like every respawn after it. You reached the
Mark chamber last time and lingered in its warm light: you are the kneeling figure at the end of the demo (§6). On the
very first pilgrimage you reached the Flame and tried to put it out with your hands, and you are still kneeling there
(Chapter V). Some of the Mason is made of you: your bodies from every earlier descent.

### 1.6 The five truths and their proof images

Rule: every truth is carried by **at least three independent images or sounds**. One is unmissable on the main route;
the others are optional rewards for players who look around. If a playtest shows a truth is not landing, we add an
image, never text.

| Truth (chapter) | Unmissable proof | Optional proofs |
|---|---|---|
| 1. The statues are not carvings (I) | Your first death leaves a stone body that looks exactly like the statues around you | A fallen statue in S3, split open, with mineral bones inside and gauntlets like yours on its wrist. Two of the 13 statues wear your gauntlets. |
| 2. They could not die, and they built with their dead (II) | The Waking Hall: thousands of lamps, each above a bed, each with a tally plate | The Dressing Yards: half-cut blocks with a lantern-arm still raised out of them. A relief of a figure falling and the same figure standing at a lamp. |

| 4. Some of them found a way to stop (IV) | **The Drain**: the water falls away to reveal thousands of the Unkindled lying at rest, and the Mason walks through them without touching one | The altar where lanterns were put out. Dark lanterns floating on the Mere like lilies. |

## 2. Storytelling rules for VR (the dread grammar)

1. **The player's body is the camera.** No cutscenes, no fades into scripted scenes, no forced camera moves, nothing
   that rotates the view. Set-pieces happen around a player who keeps full control. "Look here" is done with sound,
   light or a knock behind your head, never by taking control.
2. **Heard before seen.** Every threat is audible at least 2 s before it is visible. The Mason is fully on screen for no
   more than about 10 % of a chapter's playtime, measured in playtests (§11, N18).
3. **The betrayed safety.** Each chapter teaches one safety and then breaks it once (table in §0). **The rest space after
   every set-piece is never betrayed.** If the player cannot trust any refuge, dread turns into numbness.
4. **Silence means something different in each chapter.** I: it is listening. II: the town is holding its breath.
   III: the Kept are held. IV: you are under water and safe from its hearing. V: you are being stilled. Breath and
   heartbeat never drop out, so silence never sounds like a bug.
5. **Never the same scare twice.** No scripted scare repeats. Repeat encounters come from systems (the Mason, the Kept,
   the water), and those play out differently each time.
6. **Minimal text.** A plaque has at most 12 words, and a room has at most one. A hint appears once, and only for a new
   verb. There are no notes, diaries or voice-over. In-world text for the whole game stays **under 900 words**
   (plaques about 200, Names codex about 300, hints about 300, end cards about 100).
7. **One palette, one sound bed, one emotion per chapter.** A chapter never borrows another chapter's key colour, except
   in its last section, which previews the next palette as a promise.
8. **A ritual of rest.** After every set-piece comes at least 60 s with a waking lamp, no threat and one story image.
9. **Readable under every palette.** Anchors stay phosphor-marked and shape-coded (ring, hook, cracked diamond) in every
   chapter. In the white Chapter V they get dark iron rims, so shape and contrast carry them rather than colour.
10. **Collision is boxes; looks can be anything.** Physics is sphere-vs-AABB. Round pits, slanted stairs and figures are
    non-colliding decor around axis-aligned collision. Movers only **translate**; nothing the player stands on rotates.

## 3. The story mechanics (four systems that carry the plot)

### 3.1 Waking lamps (diegetic checkpoints): Chapter I onward

- Every checkpoint is a **votive lamp**. It kindles as you pass within 4 m (flare, a soft chime, a haptic double
  pulse), which replaces the current invisible checkpoint. Mid-section lamps let later chapters place checkpoints every
  60–90 s of median play.
- **Respawn is a waking.** You appear beside the lamp, your lantern catches from it, and you hear a breath. The death
  panel's button reads "Wake". The run stats count "Woken N times".
- **The very first frame of the game is a waking.** You stand in the dark at the Threshold holding a dead lantern. The
  lamp beside you flares and your lantern catches. Nobody notices this as a story beat until Chapter V.
- Difficulty in the fiction: Normal lights every lamp. Nightmare lights every other lamp (`checkpoints: 'alternate'`).
  Abyss leaves the lamps cold, so you wake only at the Threshold (`'none'`).

### 3.2 Remnants (your bodies stay): Chapter I onward

- Each death leaves your **stone body** where it happened, in one of five poses: lying, kneeling, reaching, hanging on
  rebar, or curled. It is hooded, carries a dark lantern and wears gauntlets identical to the player's. It does not
  collide and never blocks an anchor.
- A death to the Mason leaves no body: *"It took you."* Instead, one extra figure-plate appears in the Mason's body for
  the rest of the chapter: *it wears you now.*
- From Chapter III on, remnants in the dark **turn to face you** between glances. On NG+ they also shift closer. They
  are never lethal; they exist only for dread.
- Limits: up to 12 visible per chapter (the oldest crumble to dust), 24 per chapter persisted. Off in Sandbox. A
  "Leave no remnants" toggle exists for speedrunners and performance.
- In Abyss, which restarts from the Threshold, remnants accumulate along the shaft as a **trail of your attempts**: a
  haunting record for runners.

### 3.3 Knocking and Names: Chapter I onward

- **Knock** by tapping a wall with a controller (a quick tap of at least 1.1 m/s into the surface, with a sharp haptic
  and a stone knock spatialised at the hand). On desktop, **K** knocks the surface within 1.6 m along the view.
- A **Name** is a rhythm: 2–4 groups of 1–4 quick knocks with a pause between groups, carved as **groups of notches**
  (shape, not colour). Example: `| ||| ||` means one knock, pause, three knocks, pause, two knocks.
- **Collecting a Name.** Find the notch carving beside a statue, door or body, then knock it on that object. Its dark
  lantern flares for one breath and a tonal knock answers. The rhythm is etched onto your wrist (wrist HUD), and the
  codex gains one line from that Lanternwright (≤ 12 words).
- **Knock walls** knock at you from inside, and answering them does something: an echo behind your head (I), a door
  opening (II), or the Mason hearing you (everywhere; a knock is loud).
- **Your own Name** is knocked to you by the kneeling figure at the end of the demo, pulsing through your own
  controller as if your hand were knocking. It is also carved on the first pilgrim's gauntlets in Chapter V, so it
  cannot be missed for good.
- 25 Names (5 per chapter) plus your own unlock ending C (§7). Names carry across difficulties and survive the
  purchase.
- Accessibility option (owner call, §15): "Trace Names". Holding grip on a carving for 2 s learns it, and a learned
  Name can be played back from the wrist menu.

### 3.4 The hooded lantern: Chapter III onward

- At the Keeping's door, a **keeper's hood** hangs on a hook. Touching it clips it onto your lantern; there is no
  inventory. From then on the lantern is a **cone** (Normal: 60° full angle, 16 m range) plus a 3 m fill around your
  hands.
- **Light holds the Kept.** Anything in the cone with line of sight is still. In VR the lantern is your **left hand**,
  which is also a tether hand. Swinging on the right rope while holding a statue still with the left hand is the new
  skill. On desktop the lantern follows the view.
- **The flame leans.** Your lantern's flame leans toward the nearest unlit, moving Kept within 10 m, a diegetic
  direction cue with a faint tick in that hand's haptics. In Chapter V the flame stops flickering as you are stilled.

Chapter-local mechanics (moving anchors and lifts, water, stillness, the Mason's new behaviours) are introduced in the
chapters that use them (§5).

## 5. Chapter by chapter

Conventions: each chapter is its own `Level3D` in chapter-local coordinates, with spawn near y = 0, and a
`depthOffset` keeps the depth readout continuous across chapters. Minutes are for a median first-time player on Normal,
deaths included. Int. is intensity from 1 (calm) to 5 (peak). Every section keeps the existing
READ / CHOICE / COMMIT / RISK / RECOVERY / REWARD / ESCALATION authoring comments.

**How the Mason escalates across the game:**

| Ch | How you know it is there | What it does that is new | What it reveals |
|---|---|---|---|
| I | Sound, one far sighting, then pursuit | Hunts, lunges, punishes stalling | It is vast, and it lives in the architecture |
| II | **Inside the walls**: bulging plaster, dust, knocking that travels | **Changes the world**: it puts out the lights | It came for the lamps, not only for you |
| III | It is absent. Then you watch it feed. | **Gathers the Kept** into itself; can be delayed by them | Its origin. It grows visibly (more body segments). |
| IV | Ripples, and a crown breaking the surface | Hunts **from below**; breaks the weir | It ignores the truly dead, so it wants only the kindled |
| V | **Its shadow** | Goes for the Flame, not for you. Its shadow is your refuge. | It is pitiable, and partly you |

### CHAPTER II · LAMPHOLD *(556–1,150 m · 31–35 min)*

**Environment.** A round pit 110 m across, with the Lampholders' town carved into its walls: tiers of house-fronts,
stairs, bridges spanning the void, lamp-chandeliers on chains, and counterweight lifts. **About 3,000 small lamps are
still burning**, because lamps kindled from the Still Flame never burn down. Tables are set and beds are made. Everyone
is gone. (Collision is an octagon of axis-aligned wall boxes; the round look is decor facades.)

**Mood.** Homesickness and false warmth: the first warm place in the game, and it is a lie. The Lampholders broke the
Keepers' law (*"BELOW THIS MARK NO LAMPS"*), and their lights drew the deep course up.

**New mechanics.**
- **Knocking as a lock.** The Street of Doors teaches knocking with no danger. Doors carry notch-Names; knock one and it
  opens a shortcut or an alcove. Plaque: *"KNOCK, AND BE KNOWN."* (the Lampholders' answer to the Keepers' warning).
- **Moving anchors.** Lamp-chandeliers on pendulum paths (anchor points moving on an arc) and crane hooks on rails. The
  rope uses the anchor's live position and velocity.
- **Lifts.** Counterweight pairs (vertical translating platforms; one rises as the other falls, and you can ride either).
- **Light zones.** Clusters of lit lamps make `safeLight` volumes the Mason cannot enter. Taught in the Dressing Yards,
  then broken in the Snuffing.
- **Moving hazards.** Stone saws (hazard boxes on rails) in the Dressing Yards, telegraphed by sound.

**The Snuffing, mechanically.** The Mason follows a scripted path (`path` mode, still lethal within kill radius) and
puts out lamps within 6 m of its head. The snuff front is rubber-banded to stay 25–40 m above the player (1.2–4 m/s),
so a slow player feels it closing and a fast one stays ahead. When it is within 20 m of you in the dark, it switches to
hunting. There are three routes down the pit: a chandelier chain (fast), the lifts (safe but timed) and the stair-tiers
(slow, with two Names). A checkpoint lamp sits at the start; checkpoints inside the set-piece are the lift landings.

**Creature beats.** In the walls (heard, never seen) → one breach held back by the light → the Snuffing → the lift
chase → it withdraws once the town is dark (it did what it came to do).

**Other dangers.** The saws, the dark itself, and falls in a 110 m pit.

### CHAPTER III · THE KEEPING *(1,150–1,700 m · 32–36 min)*

**Environment.** A vertical canyon of shelves. The Keepers stored their dead here before they began building with them:
niches cut into cliff faces, racks, iron cages on chains, cranes, walkways, a central void 40 m wide. Thousands of stone
figures stand in rows. Keeper shrines with lit lamps are safe islands. Bone, ochre and black; the only warm light is
yours.

**Mood.** Paranoia and the unseen. **The Mason is absent for two thirds of the chapter.** The danger is the Kept: the
first chapter where the Mason is not the threat.

**New mechanics.**
- **The hooded lantern** (§3.4) and **the Kept**: stone figures on authored rails (shelf ledges, walkways, bridges). Out
  of your light they move toward you in short grinding steps (Normal: 0.32 m every 0.35 s, about 0.9 m/s). In your light
  they stop dead and make no sound. They hold still for a 0.3 s grace after the light leaves them. Grabs: on Normal a
  first grab staggers you, breaks one tether and holds you for 1.2 s, and a second grab within 6 s kills (*"It held you
  still."*). On Nightmare and Abyss the first grab kills.
- **Held rings.** Some Kept hold a ring aloft. The ring moves with the statue, so it is a moving anchor **you steer with
  light**: let it walk toward you in the dark, light it to freeze it in reach, fire, swing. Hang from a held ring and let
  your light slip, and it carries you, slowly, toward wherever it wants to go.
- **Cages.** Swinging cages are moving anchors with Kept inside. Unlit occupants reach through the bars.

### CHAPTER IV · THE MERE OF THE UNKINDLED *(1,700–2,250 m · 31–35 min)*

**Environment.** A flooded cavern 200 × 140 m with a black lake, the Mere, about 25 m deep. Thousands of **dark
lanterns float** on it. Chains hang from a ceiling of stalactites carved into lantern shapes. A weir dams the far end.
Somewhere under the water lie the Unkindled.

**Mood.** Mourning and near-silence: the most beautiful and quietest chapter, which is exactly why its betrayal lands.

**New mechanics.**
- **Water.** A water volume with a surface height. A fall into water at least 3 m deep is never lethal on Normal (4 m on
  Nightmare, 5 m on Abyss), and this is taught as a mercy. Swimming is slow (2.2 m/s). **The rope is the fast lane under
  water**: reeling works at full speed along submerged chains and rings. Breath lasts 20 s (14 / 10), with air pockets at
  most 15 s apart. Under water, sound is muffled and **the Mason cannot hear you**. Splashes are loud.
- **Bobbing and drifting platforms.** Rafts translate gently, never rotate, and creak when landed on (noise).
- **Current.** A current volume that pushes swimmers (up to 3 m/s), used in the Drain.
- **The altar (optional).** Hold your lantern over the altar bowl for 2 s to put it out and begin the **Lampless
  challenge**: the rest of the chapter by phosphor light only, with no waking (a death returns you to the altar). The
  reward is a Name and the Unkindled mark. *"HERE WE PUT OUT OUR LIGHTS, AND SLEPT."*

### CHAPTER V · THE STILL FLAME *(2,250–2,700 m · 30–36 min)*

**Environment.** The oldest place. Cathedral stonework gives way to rough-hewn steps, then to bare rock, so the order's
history plays in reverse as you go down. Below is a ravine where a waterfall and a rockfall hang **frozen in mid-air**.
At the bottom is **the Halo**: a spherical cavern 160 m across, filled with stilled debris and with Lanternwrights stilled
mid-fall, and at its centre a small flame on bare rock that does not move. **A hooded figure kneels at it with its hands
cupped around the light, wearing your gauntlets.**

**Mood.** Sacred awe, then terror, then quiet. Colour drains section by section (saturation 1.0 to 0.15) and sound
drains to breath and heartbeat.

**New mechanics.**
- **Stillness.** Direct line of sight to the Flame builds `stillness` (full in 12 s on Normal, 9 on Nightmare, 7 on
  Abyss; three times faster within 3 m of the Flame). Shade lets it fall away in 3 s. As it builds, reel and swing
  weaken, tether range shrinks, the gauntlets grey into stone, sound dims and **the heartbeat slows**: the threat cue
  runs in reverse. At full, you die: *"You held still."*
- **Shade.** Shade comes from solid cover, or from **the Mason's body**: its shadow is a set of capsules around its spine
  (§11, N10).
- **Stilled debris.** Frozen rock and water are solid platforms and anchors while lit. When the Mason's shadow sweeps
  over them they **unfreeze and fall**, and if you are on them you fall with them. Stilled pieces have a pale rim; one
  that is about to fall loses its rim, with a crack 0.6 s ahead and the shadow's edge visibly approaching.

**Names (5).** The tally wall, a relief on the First Steps, a frozen Lanternwright in the Stilled Fall, a ledge reached
only by riding falling debris, and the Halo shell. The first pilgrim's gauntlets carry **your own Name**, for anyone who
missed it at the hook.

**Variants.** Stillness 9 s / 7 s, shorter crown bows (5 s / 4 s), fewer solid shades. In Sandbox stillness is never

## 6. The demo hook: THE KNEELING ONE (end of Chapter I)

The free demo has to end on something nobody wants to stop at. This is one unbroken in-engine sequence of about 3 minutes
in which the player never loses control. Beats are triggered by where the player goes, not by a timer, so a player who
rushes cannot miss the key image.

1. **The slam (existing).** You sprint through the grate and it slams behind you. The Mason's crown hits the bars, its
   fronds (raised, empty arms) reach through, then withdraw. You hear it climbing away. **Relief.** The run timer stops
   here, so speedruns are unaffected.
2. **False safety (20–60 s).** The chamber is warm and the Mark Lamp burns with a flame that does not move. A hush
   volume mutes the shaft, so for the first time in the game **it is quiet**. Your heartbeat slows. Statues kneel at
   the walls.
3. **The kneeling one.** Before the lamp, with its back to you, kneels a hooded stone figure with its lantern raised,
   dark. It sits on the only path across the room. Within 3 m you can see **its gauntlets are yours**: the same mesh as
   your hands, the same tether rig, a frayed rope. If you stand close to the lamp for long, the edges of your own
   gauntlets begin to grey (a first, unexplained taste of stillness).
4. **The knock.** From inside the figure come knocks in a rhythm, **and your own controller pulses in the same rhythm**,
   as if your hand had knocked. Knock it back on the figure and its dark lantern glows for one breath: **your Name** is
   learned. This is optional and there is no prompt.
5. **The lamp goes out.** When you have been within 3 m of the figure (or after 45 s), the flame that has never moved
   **leans**, down toward the floor, as if something far below were drawing it. Then it goes out. Darkness, except your
   lantern. The hush breaks: **knocking from every wall of the chamber**, many rhythms at once.
6. **It stands.** Swing your lantern back to the figure: **it is standing.** Look away, look back: it is closer, its
   empty hand raised toward your light. While you keep it lit, it does not move. This is a scripted, non-lethal Kept: it
   always stops 1.2 m from you. It is the clip people will share.
7. **Down.** The lamp's plinth drops with a clank and a round well opens beside it. A phosphor ring lights on the cradle
   chain. If the player has not moved for 15 s, a one-word hint appears: "Down." Fire and lower yourself. Above, at the
   lip, the standing figure leans over with its arm raised. As your light leaves it, you hear it take one step.
8. **Lamphold.** The well opens into the ceiling of a vast round pit with **thousands of warm lamps** in tiers below:
   a whole town, lit. Wonder and warmth after 25 minutes of dark. Faintly, inside the walls of the pit, something large
   moves through the stone.
9. **The end card (demo build).** A carved stone panel floats at hand height on the chain (pointer UI) while the chain
   keeps lowering slowly, so the town keeps playing as the advert behind it:
   *"I · THE UNDERSPIRE · 556 m"*, your time, *"Woken N times"* (*"Woken 0 times: the lamps never had to wake you"* for a
   deathless run), Names x / 5.
   *"This was not the bottom. II Lamphold · III The Keeping · IV The Mere · V The Still Flame. 2,700 m. About two and a
   half hours."*
   **[Continue the descent: $9.99]** · [Replay Chapter I: Normal / Nightmare / Abyss] · [Menu]
10. **After purchase: seamless.** The panel crumbles to dust and you keep lowering into Lamphold's first section. Chapter
    II's data was prefetched during the hook, so there is no loading screen. Owners of the full game never see the end
    card; instead a carved title *"II · LAMPHOLD"* drifts up out of the dark.

**Why it sells.**
- It shows the player something about themselves (*those are my hands*), which no trailer can do.
- It delivers the best scare in the demo without a jump scare: a statue that moves only when you are not lighting it.
- It flips the ending players expected (*you reached the bottom*) into a beginning, and shows a new world, warm and lit,
  as the thing you are buying.
- It ends on two open questions, *who was kneeling* and *what is in that town's walls*, and the purchase answers both
  without breaking the flow.

**What the free demo contains.** All of Chapter I on Normal, Nightmare, Abyss and Sandbox, best times, 5 Names plus your
own, remnants, and the hook. Everything in the save carries over after purchase.

## 8. Progression and replay

- **Structure.** Linear chapters with waking lamps every 60–90 s of median play. Chapter select unlocks each chapter as
  you reach it. After the first ending, **Return to the Flame** starts at the Chapter V choice, so the other endings
  take minutes, not hours.
- **Names (25 plus your own).** About two per chapter are on or next to the main route; the rest reward the risk route,
  an optional detour during a set-piece, or a mechanic used cleverly (a held ring carried somewhere unusual). The pause
  menu holds a **codex**: each Name's notches plus one line. Sample lines (original):
  - *MAB TWICE-FALLEN. Fell from the Long Swing ninety times. Laughed at every waking.* (I)
  - *WENNA. Lamplighter of the Ninth Gallery. Closed it herself.* (I)
  - *BRASK. Dressed four hundred stones. Knew the last one.* (II)
  - *ENNIS. Asked to be left in the dark. We did not let him.* (III)
  - *HOB AND HALLAM. Went down to mend the deep course.* (III)
  - *IDE. Put out her light first. The rest followed her to the water.* (IV)
  - *THE FIRST. The notches are worn away.* (V; this one is your own Name)

  Read in order, the 25 lines tell the order's decline, from laughter to fear to rest.
- **Endings (3).** Recorded in the save; the menu scene changes (§7).
- **Unwoken.** Finish a chapter without dying, on any difficulty, and the chapter's lamp in chapter select burns
  steady. All five unlock a cosmetic: your lantern's flame never flickers.
- **Lampless** (Chapter IV altar). An optional no-waking challenge with its own Name.
- **Difficulties per chapter**: Normal, Nightmare, Abyss and Sandbox, with best times. **Full Descent**: all five
  chapters as one timed run (hooks and title cards skipped), unlocked after the first ending.
- **NG+ "The Waking"** *(post-launch candidate; cut first if scope is tight)*: remnants persist across the whole save
  and shift closer in the dark, statues turn to face you, and the demo's kneeling figure is missing from the chamber
  floor.

**N1. Chapter framework.** `CHAPTERS = [{id, title, dataModule, depthOffset, entitlement: 'free'|'full'}]`. Each
chapter is its own `Level3D`. `Game3D.loadChapter(i, {seamless, carry: {x,y,z,vx,vy,vz, tethers}})` keeps the player's
state across a chapter boundary. The hook geometry (chamber well and lowering chain) lives in a shared module that both
I and II load, so after purchase the swap happens while you hang. II's module is prefetched during the hook; I's GPU
geometry is disposed after the swap. Save: `{chapterReached, names[], ownName, endings, unwoken[ch][diff], totals
{deaths, wakes}, remnants[ch]}`.

**N2. Waking lamps.** Data `lamps: [{id, x,y,z, standAt:{x,y,z,yaw}, section, radius: 4, litIn: ['normal', …]}]`.
Passing within `radius` kindles the lamp, which becomes the checkpoint (it replaces the per-section `checkpointFor` and
allows several per section). Events `lamp:kindle {id}` and `player:wake {lampId}`. The renderer swaps the lamp's
emissive state and ramps the lantern from 0 on wake. Abyss lamps are cold (`litIn` excludes it).

**N3. Remnants.** On `player:death`, push `{x,y,z, cause, chapter, section, pose}`. Pose is chosen from the cause and
state: lying (fall), impaled (hazard), hanging, kneeling; a creature death adds `creature.extraPlates++` instead. Cap
12 visible and 24 persisted per chapter (Storage, under 2 KB). The view is one InstancedMesh per pose. From Chapter III,
`update(dt, player, lightField)` rotates unlit remnants to face the player while they are outside the head's view cone.
The data never touches physics.

**N4. Kinematic movers.** `movers: [{id, targets: {solids:[], anchors:[], volumes:[], hazards:[]}, path: {type: 'linear'
| 'pendulum' | 'rail' | 'bob' | 'keys' | 'fall', …}, clock: 'section' | 'trigger', loop, stillable}]`.
- Translation only. Moved boxes keep their size; `Level3D` keeps moved items in a small dynamic list that queries scan
  linearly (at most about 64 per section), so the y-bucket index is not rebuilt each step.
- `Physics3D`: a grounded player inherits the platform's velocity (`player.groundSolid.vx/vy/vz`). Moving contacts push
  the sphere out along the minimum axis, and a crush between a mover and a static solid kills (`'crush'`).
- `Rope3D` reads the anchor's live position each step. When taut, the rope's constraint uses the anchor's velocity, so
  momentum transfers both ways. Fragile and life rules still apply.
- The `fall` path applies gravity to a stilled mover once it is shaded (N11).

**N5. Volumes.** `volumes: [{id, kind, box, …}]`, queried once per physics step:
- `water` `{surfaceY (mover-driven), safeDepth}`: buoyancy (neutral 1 m under the surface, idle float up), drag
  2.5 / s, swim intent (stick, jump = up, brake = down, 2.2 m/s), lethal-impact cancel if the depth below the entry
  point is at least `safeDepth`, a breath timer while the head is under (death `'drown'`), splash `noise:emit`, an audio
  low-pass of 600 Hz while submerged, and creature hearing ×0.1.
- `current` `{vx,vy,vz}`: applies acceleration to bodies inside it.
- `hush` `{duck}`: ducks ambience and creature audio and lowers threat (false-safety rooms).
- `safeLight` `{lampIds[]}`: the creature cannot enter; it switches off when its lamps are snuffed.
- `dark` `{ambient, fogFar}`: overrides the section theme inside it.

**N6. Light field and hooded lantern.** `LightField.isLit(x,y,z)` is true if the point is inside the lantern cone (the
Game passes `lanternPose {x,y,z, dx,dy,dz, halfAngle, range}` each frame, from the left grip in XR or the camera on
desktop) with line of sight (`level.segmentBlocked`), or inside a lit static `holds` radius (shrines, lamps). Results
are cached per entity for 0.1 s, with at most 32 queries per frame. The renderer gains a hooded variant: a cone
SpotLight plus a small fill PointLight, no shadows, keeping today's fixed light count. The lantern flame leans toward
`lightField.nearestMovingUnlit`.

**N7. The Kept.** `kept: [{id, x,y,z, yaw, rail: [[x,y,z], …] | box, speed, stepLen, reach: 1.0, lethal, holds: anchorId
| null, group}]`.
- States: `STILL` (lit, or within the 0.3 s grace), `SHIFTING` (unlit: step toward the rail point nearest the player
  every `stepLen / speed` s; twice as fast while also outside the head's view cone), `REACHING` (within reach).
- Events `kept:step` (HRTF grind), `kept:grab` (stagger, forced release of the nearest tether, a 1.2 s hold; a kill by
  difficulty rule, cause `'kept'`), and `kept:gathered`.
- An anchor with `holds` follows the statue's hand (a kinematic anchor, via N4's live-anchor path).
- At most 24 active per section; beyond 30 m from the player they freeze, for CPU.

**N8. Knocks and Names.**
- *Detector (XR).* Each frame, for each hand, cast the segment from the previous grip pose to the current one against
  solids (at most 0.3 m). A hit with inward speed of at least 1.1 m/s, with at least 70 % of the hand's velocity along
  the inward normal, emits `player:knock {x,y,z, hand, solidId, speed}`, with a 0.12 s debounce per hand. Desktop: K
  raycasts 1.6 m along the view.
- *Rhythm matcher.* Knocks less than 0.45 s apart form a group. A gap of 0.55–1.3 s starts a new group. 1.5 s of
  silence ends the sequence. The result is compared to `names: [{id, chapter, pattern: [1,3,2], at: {solidId |
  decorIndex}, codex}]` with the difficulty's tolerance. Emits `name:learned`, `door:open`, `knockwall:answered`.
- *Knock walls.* `knockWalls: [{id, x,y,z, pattern, interval, respond: 'echo' | 'door' | 'creature'}]` play their
  rhythm as a spatial emitter while the player is within 12 m.
- Every knock is also `noise:emit` with loudness 0.6.
- Knock surfaces are placed at chest height within 0.6 m of a standing area, so no knock ever asks the player to reach
  into real-world space.

**N9. Noise events.** A single `noise:emit {x,y,z, loudness 0..1, kind}` from landings, yanks, knocks, splashes,
crumbles and raft creaks. Creature3D hears within `loudness × 40 m` (×0.1 under water, ×0 inside a `hush` or masking
volume such as the Breach waterfall), feeding the existing listen and lunge logic.

**N10. Creature v2** (new section modes and behaviours, all optional per section):
- `wall`: travels inside solids tagged `hollow` without being drawn. It outputs audio plus `wall:bulge {x,y,z}` events
  (dust and plaster FX) and emerges at `breach` solids, which go inactive and spill debris. *(Ch I audio-only, Ch II.)*
- `path {points, speed, lethal, rubberBand: {min, max, lead}}`: a scripted spline that is still lethal by proximity. Used
  for the Snuffing, the Gathering, the Drain and the Halo circle.
- `snuff {radius}`: puts out lamps near its head, emitting `lamp:snuff {id}`. N5 drops the matching `safeLight`.
- `safeLight` repulsion: steering targets are pushed outside safe volumes, and a player inside one is treated as
  unreachable (it waits at the edge, listening).
- `gather {radius: 6, pause: 2.5}`: absorbs Kept within range, emitting `kept:gathered` and growing `body.extraPlates`
  and spine length.
- `submerged {volumeId}`: crawls the floor of a water volume and breaches at players hanging within 4 m of the surface
  for more than 3 s, or at splashes.
- `ignore {group}`: a scripted path weaves through the Unkindled without contact.
- `bow {interval, duration}`: in the finale, coils around a point and periodically lowers its crown.
- **Body length per instance** (today `SEG = 20` is a module constant): 20 (I), 24 (II), 24 → 30 at the Gathering
  (III), 30 (IV), 36 (V). CreatureView supports a variable segment count and figure-plates.
- **Shadow export**: `creature.shadowBlocks(x0,y0,z0, x1,y1,z1)` tests a segment against capsules around each spine
  point (radius 3.5 m × scale), up to 36 capsules. Used by N11.

**N11. Stillness.** `stillSources: [{x,y,z, range, rate}]`. Each step: if the segment from the source to the player is
not blocked by solids and not shadowed by the creature, `player.stillness += rate × dt × (dist < 3 ? 3 : 1)`; otherwise
it decays at 1/3 per s. Multipliers: reel and pump × (1 − 0.6 s), tether range × (1 − 0.3 × max(0, s − 0.6) / 0.4).
`ThreatSystem3D` inverts the heartbeat as stillness rises. At 1.0, death `'still'`. A mover with `stillable: true` freezes
while it has line of sight to a source and switches to its `fall` path while shaded (with a 0.6 s crack telegraph
emitted as an event).

**N12. Sequences.** `sequences: [{id, start: {trigger | proximity | event}, steps: [{at, do: 'lamp.snuff' |
'lamp.light' | 'mover.start' | 'volume.level' | 'creature.mode' | 'creature.path' | 'solid.active' | 'sound' | 'hint',
args}]}]`. Every sequence's state is snapshotted when a lamp is kindled and restored exactly on waking. This drives the
Nave's false lulls, the hook, the Snuffing's scripted layer, the Gathering, the Drain and the endings.

**N13. Hand interactions.** `interactables: [{id, x,y,z, radius, hands: 'any' | 'both' | 'left', grip: bool, hold: s,
onComplete: event}]`. Hand-pose proximity plus grip held for `hold` seconds. Desktop fallback: look within 10° and
within 1.5 m, then hold E (or F for the lantern). Used for the keeper's hood, the altar, and cupping or kindling the
Flame.

**N14. Renderer extensions.**

## 12. Audio and haptics needs

heard only while it moves, and the Flame is heard as the absence of everything.

## 14. Risks and mitigations

| Risk | Why it matters | Mitigation |
|---|---|---|
| **Scope**: four new chapters, about 2 h of content | Content is the critical path (RELEASE.md §17) | Shared figure kit across five uses; a few systems reused across chapters; a cut list in order: NG+ → Lampless → cages (III) → lifts (II, keep chandeliers) → the Breach waterfall (IV, replace with a shaft). Never cut: the hook, the Gathering, the Drain, the Halo, the endings. |
| **Twist legibility** with under 900 words | A story nobody understands sells nothing | Three proofs per truth (§1.6). Gaze telemetry (N18). Target: at least 70 % of first-time testers can state truth 1 by the end of II, truth 3 by the end of III, and truth 5 at the Flame. If not, add an image, never text. |
| **Kept plus two tethers overloads the hands** | The left hand is both lantern and tether | Generous Normal cone (60°), 0.3 s grace, the leaning flame and haptic tick, the two-grab rule on Normal, shrines as true safe islands. Playtest early in M-C with a greybox room. Fallback: Normal also counts the head's view cone as holding light. |
| **Knock false positives** while swinging | Accidental knocks would make noise and feel unfair | Rhythm matching needs 2 or more knocks with gaps; knock walls listen only within 12 m; speed and direction thresholds (N8); knock surfaces only beside standing areas. |
| **Remnants feel punishing or clutter the view** | Bodies at every hard spot | Non-colliding, never on anchors, cap 12, oldest crumble; Sandbox off; "Leave no remnants" toggle; never lethal. |
| **Motion intensity** (no comfort options, an owner decision) with moving anchors, lifts and water bobbing | Store rating "Intense"; drop-off | Movers translate only and never rotate the view; smooth acceleration; bobbing at most 0.2 m; documented in the store rating. |
| **Sensitive themes**: bodies, "chose to sleep" | Rating, reviews, platform policy | Stone only, no gore; the Unkindled framed as rest from a curse, peaceful imagery; no children; IARC answers reviewed before submission. |
| **Performance**: 3,000 lamps, water, many figures, the bright Chapter V | Quest 3 budget (≤ 150 draws, ≤ 350k triangles) | Instancing (lamps, figures, lanterns), a single water quad per volume, Kept frozen beyond 30 m, bright fog capping draw distance in V, no post-processing (grade lives in materials). |
| **Silence reads as a bug** | Players think audio broke | Breath and heartbeat never stop; silence always comes from a visible cause (a hush room, under water, stillness). |
| **Ending discoverability** | Players miss B or C | Image signposts (§7); Return to the Flame; the Mason's pits pulsing a rhythm you don't fully know hints that C exists. |
| **Seamless hand-off memory** (two chapters loaded) | Quest memory and stalls | The hand-off module is small; textures are shared; I's GPU data is disposed after the swap; II is prefetched during the hook's quiet beats. |
| **Overlap with the other pitches** | Three different lores | This is a story spine plus a dread grammar; it can host the other pitches' mechanics (§15). |

## 15. Open questions for the owner

1. **Respawn as story.** Do we adopt the "waking" premise? It changes checkpoint presentation in Chapter I (lamps), the
   death screen wording and the demo's ending. It is the core of this pitch.
2. **Remnants on by default?** (Recommended: yes, with the toggle.)
3. **Ending C gate:** all 25 Names, or a lighter 20? (Recommended: all 25. It is the replay hook.)
4. **"Trace Names" accessibility option** for players who cannot knock in rhythm. This is about motor access, not
   locomotion comfort, so it does not conflict with the no-comfort-options decision.
5. **A commissioned original score** for the title screen and the three endings only (gameplay stays procedural and
   music-free).
6. **Merging the pitches.** This story can absorb the set-piece pitch's machinery (as the Dressing Yards in II) and the
   systems pitch's live anchors (Lamphold's chandeliers and lifts) without changing its truths, endings or hook.
